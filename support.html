Common questions

What does Notched Music do?

Notched helps you find the pitch of your tinnitus, then plays your own music with a narrow band around that pitch removed (a “notch”). Listening to notched music regularly is an approach studied in tinnitus research. Results vary from person to person.

How do I find my tinnitus frequency?
  • Use the Finder tab in a quiet room, wearing headphones.
  • Set your volume to a comfortable level before you start.
  • Pick whichever tone sounds closer to your tinnitus at each step.
  • Repeat the test on a different day. If your results vary a lot, the app will flag the match as low confidence.
Do I need headphones?

Yes, wired or Bluetooth headphones are strongly recommended. Phone speakers and room echo make frequency matching unreliable and reduce the effect of the notch.

Which music can I use?

Notched works with DRM-free audio files you own, imported from the Files app (for example MP3, AAC/M4A, WAV, or FLAC). Songs from streaming services such as Apple Music or Spotify are copy-protected and cannot be imported.

I re-tested and got a different frequency. What happens to my songs?

Each song remembers the frequency it was processed with. After a new test, songs made with the old frequency can be processed again so they match your current profile.

How do I delete my data?

Use Settings → Delete all profiles, or delete the app to remove everything. Notched stores your data only on your iPhone, so we have no copy to delete. See the Privacy Policy.

I got a new iPhone. How do I move my data?

Notched has no account or cloud sync. Your data moves only if you restore your new iPhone from an iCloud or computer backup of your old one. You can always re-run the Finder and re-import your music.

How do I re-download Notched Music or request a refund?

Purchases are handled by Apple. You can re-download Notched Music at no cost from the App Store with the same Apple Account. To request a refund, visit reportaproblem.apple.com.

Important

Notched Music is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition. Listen at a comfortable volume. If your tinnitus changes suddenly, affects only one ear, pulses with your heartbeat, or comes with hearing loss or dizziness, see a doctor or audiologist.
